Grading scales by country

An open, sourced dataset of grade bands, percentage cutoffs and grade points for 12 countries and regions. Every row links to the public source it came from, and where a scale varies by institution the notes say so.

Download CSV Download JSON JSON Schema GitHub

There is no international grading scale. A 75% is a First in the UK, a Distinction in Australia, a B in Canada and a C at most US colleges. This dataset puts the common systems side by side in one consistent format so you can compare, convert and build on them. For a plain-English walkthrough of what the same score means in each system, see the grading scales by country guide.

Percent to grade converter

Pick a percentage-based system and enter a mark. The converter reads the same JSON file you can download.

Marks between two bands (for example 92.5 when one band ends at 92 and the next starts at 93) fall into the lower band, with no rounding. Systems without fixed percentage cutoffs, such as Germany or the CBSE positional grades, are not listed. To combine grade points across courses, use a credit-weighted GPA calculator.

United States

College letter grades (4.0 GPA, plus/minus)

Plus/minus grades sit 0.3 above or below the plain letter. Some schools use thirds (3.67, 3.33) or give an A+ 4.33; the GPA scale guide compares the conventions.

No common percentage cutoff (null): many schools do not award A+, and those that do set their own cutoff (Columbia SPS uses 98-100). Most schools count A+ as 4.0; some (e.g. Columbia SPS, Arizona State) give 4.33.

Common US college percentage cutoffs as compiled in the mygradecalculator.net GPA scale guide from Columbia University School of Professional Studies (A to C-) and the University of the Incarnate Word (D+ to D-, https://my.uiw.edu/registrar/academics/grading-scale.html); standard grade points per George Washington University (https://advising.columbian.gwu.edu/gpa-calculator). Cutoffs, plus/minus use and A+/A- point values vary by institution (e.g. some use 3.67/3.33 thirds).

Scale 1-10, used in secondary and higher education; not percentage-based (min/max_percent null). 6 is the minimum pass; in secondary education 5.5 is rounded to 6, and in higher education 5.5 is sometimes sufficient. Europe (ECTS)

ECTS grading scale A-E (relative, pre-2009)

GradeDescription
ATop 10% of passing students
BNext 25% of passing students
CNext 30% of passing students
DNext 25% of passing students
ELowest 10% of passing students

Historical relative ECTS grading scale based on the statistical distribution of passing grades in a reference group, so there are no percentage-of-marks cutoffs (min/max_percent null). The 2009 ECTS Users' Guide replaced it with the ECTS grading table, which reports the actual percentage of students receiving each local grade.

Source: ehea.info

Data format

Each row is one grade band. min_percent and max_percent are inclusive and are null for systems that are not percentage-based (the native range is in description). gpa_points uses the system's own point scale. Full details are in the README.

country, system, grade, min_percent, max_percent, gpa_points, description, source_url, notes
